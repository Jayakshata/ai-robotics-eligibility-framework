The programme survives. The documents describing it did not. The underlying proposition — a post-ITI qualification in uptime and recovery, delivered on cells running paying customer work — is real and unduplicated in any curriculum we could check. Almost everything wrapped around it was wrong in ways a selection committee could verify on a phone.

All fifteen corrections have been applied to the programme design. This page records what was found and what remains unconfirmed.

Rewrite the wedge from “real robot versus no robot” to “production cell versus training cell.” Tamil Nadu has already spent ₹2,877 crore putting an Industrial Robotics and Digital Manufacturing Lab into 71 government ITIs — and the people who will read the application are the government that paid for it. Everything else in the pitch hangs off that one sentence being defensible.

Where the research was weak — say so

The founding premise is unverified. No published study establishes that Tamil Nadu has a technician shortage rather than an engineer shortage. The only supporting evidence is qualitative. This is an assertion, not a finding — and BGR Neo's own installed-base data is the only way to convert it.

India's robot density must not be quoted. The international federation's density release excludes India and public secondary sources disagree by a factor of five. Use installations and stock instead. No technician-to-engineer staffing ratio exists in any credible source — the maintenance profession benchmarks headcount against asset value, not against engineers. Do not put a sourced-looking ratio on a slide.

Section 2What had to change

Ranked by severity. Each records what the plan said, what is actually true, and what was done. All fifteen are now applied.

The wedge was factually false in Tamil Nadu

Critical
Said
"Almost nobody can put a fresh graduate in front of a real industrial robot and a real factory integration project."
True
The state converted 71 government ITIs into Industry 4.0 Technology Centres at ₹2,877 crore, each with an Industrial Robotics and Digital Manufacturing Lab. Add a state-partnered robotics CoE inaugurated in March 2026 whose own marketing says it "closely replicates live industrial environments", an OEM centre at a Chennai engineering college, a simulation centre at Tidel Park, and a university CoE the state skill corporation itself co-funded.
Done
Replaced with production cell versus training cell — those labs have no customer, no order book, no acceptance test and no consequence for downtime. Added the second claim an OEM structurally cannot make: multi-vendor cells and cross-stack integration no single robot maker owns.

The certification route named in the plan does not exist

Critical
Said
Certification via the instrumentation-and-automation sector skill council, which "covers industrial robotics", mapping to levels 5–8.
True
That council's entire published catalogue is 16 qualifications with no robot operator, technician or programmer role. Its only robotics artefact is a standalone occupational standard, which is not a certifiable job role. Its highest published qualifications are level 5, so "5–8" was wrong. The capital-goods council is not the alternative either — all 24 of its future-skills qualifications sit at level 5.5–6.0 with degree entry, and one sets a minimum job-entry age of 24. The choice the plan posed was a false one.
Done
Rebuilt as an honest composite — an automation-technician qualification as the assessed spine with its limits stated, a robotics occupational standard bolted on, the compulsory employability standard, and a CoE certificate carrying everything a qualification cannot. Plus a standing rule: name no qualification code until the council confirms version and validity in writing — their published lists use colliding codes and at least two show review dates that lapsed in mid-2025.

The technician syllabus did not teach PLC — so two of its own seven fault domains were untaught

Critical
Said
Every cell is the same seven things; domain 4 is the Brain (PLC, controller, HMI) and domain 5 the Nerves (I/O, fieldbus); the technician is portable because they know all seven.
True
"PLC" appeared three times in the whole document — in the fault-domain table, the portability table, and the forbidden-actions list. No module taught a trainee to read a rung, open the software in monitor mode, cross-reference an alarm to logic, or read a fieldbus diagnostic page. The result was a technician told "never alter PLC logic" who was never taught to read one. The portability promise was 5/7 delivered.
Done
Added M8 — PLC & Control Logic Literacy, 60 h, read-only. Monitor mode only, which reinforces the authority boundary rather than weakening it. A parallel R6 was added to the graduate tier for the same reason: industrial cells run PLCs, not ROS 2.

The hour totals fitted no qualification band at all

High
Said
Tier 1 at 380 h ≈ level 4; Tier 2 at 380 h ≈ level 5; a combined 760-hour award.
True
The level 4 notional band is 390–480 hours — so 380 was ten hours below the floor. Level 5 requires three years' experience, or a higher school exit, so it was structurally barred for the declared flagship audience. And 760 h exceeded every short-term band from level 4 to 6.5 while sitting far below the long-term norm. The framework also wants hours in multiples of 30. It was homeless.
Done
Restructured as two separately certified level 4 awards of 450 h — on the band, 15 credits exactly, and inside the 300–600 h national funding window, so each tier is independently fundable. Repositioned honestly as "same level, scarcer competence", because a fresh two-year-trade passout already holds level 4.

The vehicle is closed, and never covered this sector

High
Said
Recommended because "it is winnable via a mechanism that already exists."
True
The only published state CoE call covered IT/ITES, animation and electronics — no robotics, no manufacturing — and closed in January 2025. The one robotics CoE the state does have was awarded by direct memorandum with CSR funding, not competition. Three further terms the plan had not absorbed: the state provides the space in a government building, per-trainee rates are decided case by case with no published card, and at least 70% placement is required and reviewed quarterly.
Done
Replaced the single recommendation with a two-door plan — near-term empanelment under an existing live scheme, and a direct memorandum on the template that actually worked. Added a national skilling mission absent from the plan entirely, and the knowledge-partner slot as the way to bring the university into the same bid.

The Handover Model did not survive its own timeline

High
Said
Two shifts on the same cell for twelve weeks; shift handover becomes "something they do 120 times."
True
The document carried three contradictory live-cell windows. 120 daily handovers is 24 working weeks — arithmetically impossible against a 12-week window. A cell under active integration cannot be handed to production every evening, yet the model required it be left "run-able at every handover". Safety re-validation after modification was uncosted against one shared safety officer. And it consumed the only cell available for the flagship unseen-cell examination.
Done
Kept the pedagogy, changed the mechanics: milestone-gated handovers at acceptance-test and production-ramp boundaries (6–8 per cohort), on a ring-fenced training cell, with a dedicated safety officer, a staffed re-validation step, and a separate assessment cell no cohort trains on. Windows resolved to one number.

The PG Diploma was over-sized — and the discipline question was never asked

High
Said
PG Diploma, one year, 1,400 hours, ~46 credits.
True
A postgraduate diploma is fixed at 40 credits — 1,200 hours. The plan was 17% over on both. Separately, the two-year rule that first appeared to block a one-year diploma turned out to apply only to Engineering & Technology under the technical regulator — not to an autonomous arts & science college under the higher-education regulator, where a one-year 40-credit diploma is ordinary business.
Done
Reduced to 1,200 h / 40 credits and routed through Loyola College, whose autonomous Academic Council approves its own curriculum. Sequencing reversed: sign the partner before or alongside filing, not after, and put them into the bid through the knowledge-partner slot.

A compulsory standard was missing, and named safety standards were absent

High
True
The spine qualification carries 60 hours of Employability Skills as one of only four compulsory standards; national trade curricula allocate 120 hours of it to every trade. Any certificate pursued will mandate it. Separately, the technician tier's 60-hour protected safety module cited no standard at all, while the graduate tier's equivalent cited two.
Done
Added 60 h of employability skills outside the trade hours, 30 per tier, mapped to the national standard — and mapped the graduate tier's portfolio module to the same. Named six standards in the safety module covering emergency-stop categories, light-curtain minimum distance and functional safety. Added the benchmark nobody was using: Japan legally mandates 10 hours of robot special education for teaching work; this module is 60 — six times the statutory floor.

The entry-trade list named a trade that does not exist in Tamil Nadu

High
Said
Direct fit includes Millwright.
True
No Millwright trade exists in Tamil Nadu. Worse, the list demoted the state's best feeder and promoted its rarest: Machine Tool Maintenance runs at 3 ITIs, Mechatronics at 10 — while Manufacturing Process Control & Automation runs at 44 and owns the exact two fault domains that were untaught. The realistic intake is Electrician and Fitter across 574 ITIs, neither of which teaches PLC, sensors, robots or automation — so the 60-hour bridge was under-scoped for the people who will actually enrol.
Done
Deleted Millwright, promoted the automation trade to priority feeder, demoted the rare ones, split entry bands by school exit (the only route to level 5), and replaced the single bridge with two streams — 60 h for prepared entrants, 120 h with an automation primer for Electrician and Fitter passouts.

Seven further corrections

Medium & below
  • Both tiers claimed to be "the volume play." Both cannot be — and against the state's own baseline, 90 a year is a rounding error. Reframed around the real opening: the state publishes intake for its 71 centres and no placement outcomes whatsoever. Compete on audited outcomes.
  • Two "open decisions" were already closed by evidence. Entry-level automation wages make a six-month course impossible to trainee-fund; and the apprenticeship scheme provides no training-cost reimbursement at all — it is an employment wrapper, not funding.
  • Missing content: hydraulics (zero occurrences, yet machine tending is a named target role), metrology (the tier is measured on first-pass yield but nothing taught gauging), and environmental handling. All three added.
  • Graduate-tier defects: an elective tagged droppable was retained anyway; the "all types of AI" breadth claim rested on two modules with no cards; and the six-station rotation was unschedulable because station dwell times were unequal. All fixed.
  • An age gate nobody had checked: both candidate qualifications for the six-month graduate product set a minimum job-entry age of 24. A cohort finishing at 22–23 cannot be certified against either. The claim was withdrawn.
  • A capex line that would be challenged: India's dock automation is telescopic conveyors, not robots, with no evidenced state installed base. The truck-loading mock-up was deleted and taught within palletizing instead. Showcase applications reweighted toward machine tending and automotive, where 45% of India's robots actually go.
  • Naming and market visibility: the programme title read as a robotics course and invited the duplication question on page one, while being commercially invisible — job boards carry roughly twenty times more postings for "PLC / automation maintenance" than for anything labelled robotics. Renamed around uptime, with a market-facing title alongside it on the certificate.

Only BGR Neo can answer these — and they are the differentiated evidence

  • Technician-to-cell ratio from the installed base — operators and maintenance heads per delivered cell per shift.
  • Post-handover support calls per cell per year, and what share a trained on-site technician could have cleared.
  • Audited turnover and net worth against the thresholds that decide which doors open — and whether the group parent would sign a financial-support undertaking.
  • Signed employer demand letters from named plants. This is the evidence a selection committee actually weights, and the one deliverable BGR Neo is uniquely positioned to produce.

Section 5How this was checked

Eight parallel research passes — national trade curricula, sector skill council catalogues, the state skilling ecosystem, industry and OEM training precedents, international models, demand evidence, funding mechanics, and the academic route. Each was required to mark every finding Verified with a source, Likely, or Unverified — and told that "I could not confirm this" was an acceptable answer while a confident wrong number was not.

Four adversarial reviews then attempted to refute the plan rather than confirm it, with their own source access, and were told that findings marked unverified carried no weight. All four returned needs change. That stage is what produced most of what is on this page; the research alone would have been too generous.

One caveat on this document

This is machine-assisted research with primary sources attached, and the adversarial pass caught real errors in the first round of findings. But before anything is filed, a person should open the source links on the highest-stakes claims — particularly the qualification codes, the funding rates, and the status of the state's own programmes. The full report with every URL and confidence level is held alongside the programme design.
