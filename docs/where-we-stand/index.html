01What changes if the CoE stands alone

Running the Centre as an independent body, separate from BGR Neo's project work, is a clean and defensible decision. It does change one thing, and it is better to know which.

Loses its force

"We train on live customer cells"

The strongest claim in the earlier material was that trainees work on a real integration project — a paying customer, an acceptance test, a real handover, and a consequence when the line stops. No training centre in India can say that.

Without the order book behind it, our cells are training cells, like everyone else's. The claim does not become false — it becomes unavailable until internships or project placements are added later.

Unaffected

Everything built into the curriculum

Four robot brands · the fault-domain method · the 48-scenario fault library · recovery time as a graded result · the authority boundary · the ITI-to-postgraduate ladder · the UGC diploma through Loyola.

None of these depend on an order book. They are design decisions, and they are what the rest of this page compares.

The practical consequence

Our differentiation now has to come from what we teach and what we award, not from where we teach it. That is a harder position to hold but a more durable one — an order book can be lost, a curriculum and a degree cannot.

An internship or project-placement arrangement with BGR Neo later restores the live-cell claim without changing anything in the Centre's structure. Keep it as a year-two option, not a founding assumption.

The two patterns in that table

Everyone who teaches, teaches one brand. Delta, FANUC, KUKA, Bosch Rexroth and Siemens all fund centres that showcase their own equipment. That is why they fund them. It is a structural limit, not an oversight — and it is the one gap nobody in the list can close.

Everyone who incubates, doesn't teach a trade. ARTPARK and IIT Madras run world-class deep-tech incubation and no shop-floor training. TANSAM is the only one attempting both, and it does it with simulation rather than production hardware.

04Where we genuinely stand

Six claims survive scrutiny. Three are contested. One is gone. Being clear about which is which is what makes the other six believable.

Ours — structural, and hard to copy

Claim 1 · Strongest
Keep and lead with

Four robot brands

FANUC, ABB, KUKA and Universal Robots, taught to operating competence — 140 hours in the technician programme.

Every other teaching centre in the comparison is funded by one manufacturer and teaches that manufacturer's equipment. They cannot copy this without giving up their funding. It is the single most defensible thing we have.

Claim 2
Keep and lead with

A real qualification, not a certificate

A one-year, 40-credit UGC Postgraduate Diploma awarded by Loyola College.

Everyone else issues attendance or vendor certificates. A postgraduate diploma is recognised by employers, by government schemes and by universities for further study. Nothing else in the Tamil Nadu robotics landscape offers one.

Claim 3
Keep

One ladder, ITI to postgraduate

An ITI passout can enter at the technician tier, work for a period, and return for the diploma — with credit for the content that overlaps. The 6-month Engineer Training programme sits between them as a later addition; the ladder holds without it.

Every other centre serves exactly one audience. Nobody offers a route that starts at a 10th-standard trade certificate and ends at a postgraduate award.

Claim 4
Keep

Built around uptime, not programming

Seven fault domains · a six-step fault procedure drilled to reflex · 48 named fault scenarios seeded on real cells · recovery time measured and graded · a clear boundary of what a technician may and may not touch.

No programme found in India is built this way. The nearest national qualification calls itself maintenance and spends 219 of 450 hours on programming and 17 on maintenance.

Claim 5 · New
Keep and lead with

AI is the subject, not the robot

The diploma's shared foundation is 890 hours of AI — computer vision, robot learning, reinforcement and imitation learning, LLMs, retrieval and agents — before a student picks one of five specialisations.

Read the comparison in section 2 again. Every centre there teaches robots and automation: Siemens teaches PLM and AR/VR, FANUC teaches FANUC systems, KUKA runs one welding cell, Delta teaches PLC. None of them teaches AI as the subject, and none offers a choice of specialisation under one award.

Claim 6
Keep, position carefully

Graduates are not all going to factories

Only one of the five tracks is manufacturing. Perception & Autonomy leads to defence, drones, autonomous vehicles, mining, agriculture and inspection. Robot Learning leads to research labs and robotics companies. Analytics and Agentic AI serve any industry with data.

That widens the placement pool well beyond the Tamil Nadu automotive corridor every other centre competes in. The employer and salary evidence for the four non-manufacturing tracks is not yet gathered — it needs the same sourcing the manufacturing roles had before it goes into a prospectus.

The humanoid — a visitor asset, and how to talk about it

One humanoid is in the launch build at ₹25–60 lakh. It carries no teaching hours in either programme, and it is deliberately not in the comparison table above, because it is not a competitive claim about training. It is a demonstration and research platform — and the thing a visiting official or a journalist will photograph.

Say: the Centre holds a humanoid platform for demonstration and for the frontier work it expects to be asked for. Do not say: that we train people on humanoids, or place graduates into humanoid companies. The build plan already records why the second version does not survive a recruiter — those firms hire globally and mostly want a master's with published research.

05Research, incubation, placement — tested

The idea was that offering all three together would set us apart. Tested against the landscape, that is half right: offering all three is not unusual, but the shape we give each one can be.

Pillar 1
Reframe

Research

Weak as stated. ARTPARK has ₹170 crore. IIT Madras Research Park is in our city. We cannot compete on research funding or publications.

Strong if narrowed to: an annual reliability report on Tamil Nadu automation — which subsystems fail, how often, how long recovery takes, by application. Built from our own fault library and our own graduates' field data. Nobody has this data because nobody collects it.

Pillar 2
Reframe and stage

Incubation

Weak as stated, and it has a hard entry requirement — see section 6.

Strong if narrowed to: helping graduates start automation service businesses — maintenance contracts, retrofits, commissioning support for small manufacturers. Low capital, immediate revenue, and it multiplies our placement numbers instead of competing with them.

Deep-tech venture incubation should be a referral to ARTPARK or IIT Madras, not something we attempt.

Pillar 3
Strongest of the three

Placement

Strong, if we publish. Everyone promises 70%. Nobody publishes what actually happened.

Commit to publishing, every year: placement rate, median starting salary, six-month retention, and employer names. Audited. It costs almost nothing and it is the one claim a government committee can verify — which is exactly why nobody makes it.

A fourth pillar worth considering

The ARM Institute in Pittsburgh does not mainly deliver training — it endorses other people's training against a national standard. Japan legally requires robot safety training before a worker may touch an industrial robot; India requires nothing.

A state-backed Centre could propose becoming the body that sets and certifies that standard for Tamil Nadu. It is a bigger ask than running courses, it costs little, and it would put us above every centre in section 2 rather than beside them.

06What incubation really requires

If the word "incubation" is going to appear in the proposal, it should mean something specific. The national scheme has firm entry conditions.

The applicant must be an academic, technical or R&D institution with a track record in promoting technology entrepreneurshipNot as a company. Yes if Loyola College is the host institution and we are the industry partner — this is the unlock

The honest recommendation on incubation

Stage it. Year one, run a graduate venture support desk — mentoring, workspace, tooling access and help with registration for graduates starting automation service firms. It costs very little and produces stories.

Apply for formal incubator recognition only in year two or three, with Loyola as the host institution and a real track record behind the application. Claiming formal incubation in year one, in the same city as IIT Madras, invites a comparison we would lose.

08The position, in one paragraph

Say this

Every robotics training centre in Tamil Nadu is funded by one equipment manufacturer and teaches that manufacturer's robots, and every one of them issues a certificate. We teach four brands, we award a postgraduate diploma through Loyola College, and we run a single ladder from an ITI passout to a postgraduate qualification. We train people to keep automated plants running — measured on how fast they recover a stopped cell — and we will publish what happens to our graduates, which nobody in the state currently does.

What to claim, and what to leave out

Lead with

Four claims

  • Four robot brands — nobody else can
  • A UGC postgraduate diploma — nobody else has one
  • One ladder from ITI to postgraduate — nobody else spans it
  • Built around uptime and recovery time — nobody else is
Do not lead with

Three claims

  • Research — until it is narrowed to the reliability report
  • Incubation — until Loyola is the host and there is a track record
  • Live customer cells — until an internship arrangement exists

Each of these becomes usable later. Claiming them early is what a committee tests first, and what a competitor points at.
